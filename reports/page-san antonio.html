What it is actually like to play here

Parking in San Antonio

San Antonio says something about parking, which is more than most parks departments do, and still not very much. Seven of the eight park pages list "Regular Parking Spaces" and "Handicapped Parking Spaces" among their amenities - Fairchild, Garza, Monterrey, Normoyle, Oak Haven, Pittman-Sullivan and Tejeda. That is a statement that a lot exists and that accessible bays are marked, and it is the whole of it: no capacity, no fee, no permit, and no note on where the lot sits relative to the courts. Palm Heights Park's amenity list has no parking line at all, and this site records that as a silence rather than a no, since the same list omits restrooms too on a 1.72-acre park with a community centre and gymnasium. Restrooms are the other thing to plan around. The City writes "Restrooms" at Normoyle, Pittman-Sullivan and Tejeda, and "Portable Toilet" at Garza, Monterrey and Oak Haven; Fairchild and Palm Heights list neither. Four parks - Garza, Normoyle, Palm Heights and Pittman-Sullivan - list Wi-Fi. What none of the eight pages describes is the walk from the car to the courts, and at Monterrey, 51 acres with soccer fields, softball fields and a community centre, that is not a trivial omission. One address quirk to know before you drive: Oak Haven's page prints "16400 Parkstone" under its pickleball entry and "2215 Rest Haven Dr." in its contact block. The City's directory uses Rest Haven, and so does this site.

When the courts are busy

San Antonio publishes one clock for all eight venues and it is a generous one. Every park on the City's pickleball directory carries the same line, "Park Hours: Sunday - Saturday: 5 a.m. - 11 p.m.", and the directory states the rule that connects the two: "All other courts are available during park hours." Fairchild Park's own page repeats it for its courts specifically - "All tennis courts and pickleball courts are open during park hours." So the window is eighteen hours a day, seven days a week, at all eight parks. How much of that window is usable after dark is a different question, and the City answers it at only two venues. Fairchild's eighteen courts and Tejeda's six are "lighted" in the City's own count sentences, which is twenty-four of the forty-three verified courts. For the other six parks the directory's caveat applies: "Some courts are not lighted." That sentence is why this site records their lighting as unknown rather than reading the 11 p.m. closing time as a promise. Several of those pages do say whether the tennis court is lit - Garza's are, Monterrey's, Normoyle's and Pittman-Sullivan's are not, Oak Haven's is lit until 9 p.m. under a pilot programme - but every one of those sentences is about tennis, and this site does not carry them across to the pickleball courts alongside. Two exceptions to the flat schedule are stated. Hamilton Community Center's courts, which are not published here, are "available Saturday, 10 a.m. - 2 p.m." And Fairchild's page warns that "Special events and scheduled activities may affect court usage and open hours", with a phone number to check. Everything is first come, first served, and the City publishes no rotation rule, no league night, and nothing about when any court is busy.

Court conditions

The City of San Antonio names a surface for a pickleball court nowhere. The word "asphalt" appears on these pages three times and none of them is about pickleball: Garza's trail is "Asphalt", Monterrey's is "Decomposed Granite, Asphalt", and Palm Heights' history records that "in August 1929, a contract was signed for construction of cold rolled asphalt tennis courts" - a tennis surface, ninety-seven years ago. So all forty-three verified courts read as not stated on surface, and that is a gap in what the City writes rather than a choice of this site's. What the City does write, unusually, is where the courts are. Its count sentences carry the word "outdoor" at six parks and "indoor" at Palm Heights, which puts thirty-five of the forty-three courts outdoors and two inside, at a park whose history records a gymnasium being added in 1977. San Antonio is one of the few operators here that writes "outdoor" or "indoor" into the count sentence itself. The exception shows how literally that sentence is read. Tejeda Park's line is "There are 6 lighted, pickleball courts." - lighted, but not outdoor - and rather than complete the City's sentence for it, this site leaves Tejeda's six as neither indoor nor outdoor. Nothing on any page says whether a court is dedicated to pickleball or lined onto a tennis court; the amenity lists carry "Pickleball Court" and "Tennis Court" as separate entries and stop there.

The San Antonio pickleball scene

Forty-three verified courts across eight City parks, and the arithmetic is lopsided: Fairchild Park holds eighteen of them, Monterrey eight, Tejeda six, and the other five parks share eleven. Fairchild's eighteen lit outdoor courts are the second-largest single-venue count on this site after Mesa's Tennis & Pickleball Center, and the largest anywhere here where the operator states that every court is both lit and outdoor. Twenty-four of the forty-three are under lights, all of them at Fairchild and Tejeda; two, at Palm Heights, are the City's only indoor count. San Antonio is the second Texas city on this site after Austin and the first in Bexar County. Austin publishes twenty-one venues and sixty courts, all outdoor, with lighting stated at eleven of them; San Antonio publishes fewer venues, more courts at the top, and a lighting answer at two. What San Antonio does not say is the price. "These are available on a first-come, first-served basis" is a play format, not a fee, and nothing on the City's directory page or on any park page states a charge or the absence of one, so no venue in San Antonio is called free. The City offers a way in for beginners: "Take a Pickleball 101 class through the Fitness in the Park program, equipment provided." Two listed venues are not published. Piazza Italia Park has "one outdoor pickleball court", restrooms and park hours, and an address, "500 Columbus", that neither the US Census address file nor OpenStreetMap can find; the City writes it without a street type on both its pages. Hamilton Community Center, at 10700 Nacogdoches Rd., has facility hours, a Saturday pickleball window, and no court count anywhere. The directory page also says "11 Result(s) Found" and serves ten, and its second page returns the same ten; the eleventh is not guessed at. On the record itself: the City's site answered a plain request with 200 and, twenty minutes later, 403, so its pages are fetched with a browser header set. A 403 is a fact about one day, and every quoted sentence here is re-asserted on every build.
