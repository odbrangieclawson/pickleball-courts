What it is actually like to play here

Parking in Irvine

The City of Irvine publishes nothing about parking at any of these three parks. The word does not appear on the pickleball page, on the court regulations page or on any of the three park pages, and this site does not fill it in from a map. What each park page does carry is an amenity list, an acreage, a phone number and the hours, and those say what kind of site you are arriving at. All three are large community parks with a great deal happening besides pickleball. Mike Ward Community Park is approximately 22 acres, with two lighted basketball courts, three lighted racquetball courts, a sand volleyball court, an amphitheatre, a fitness parcourse and three restrooms. Portola Springs Community Park is approximately 32 acres, with a community centre, two lighted softball fields, two lighted soccer fields, three lighted tennis courts, a concession building and two restrooms. Heritage Community Park is the largest at approximately 36.5 acres, with a community centre, twelve lighted tennis courts, three lighted soccer fields, two lighted ball diamonds, a lake, a splash pad and four restrooms. Every one of those lit fields is a reason other people will be at the park after dark, and the City states that its sponsored programmes have priority over all other use of the courts. Restrooms are stated at all three parks. A lot, a fee, a permit or a capacity is not stated at any of them, and the gap is recorded as a gap.

When the courts are busy

Irvine states no peak times. What it states instead is a set of rules for how a court is shared, and they are the most useful thing on this page. All three parks are open 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., daily, on the City's own park pages, and every one of the eighteen courts is lit, so the window is sixteen hours long and does not shrink at dusk. Within that window the court regulations say: "Open courts are on a first-come, first-serve basis. Please use the waiting board where available." When others are waiting, doubles is limited to 60 minutes and singles to 30, warm-up included, and "all players and group members must vacate the court when the time limit expires". If nobody is waiting you may carry on. Groups "may not camp on a court". Two courts at each of the two largest venues run on a different clock. Courts #3 and #4 at Mike Ward and Courts #5 and #6 at Portola Springs can be reserved online "between the hours of 7 a.m. to noon, and 4 to 10 p.m.", in one- or two-hour blocks, one reservation per person per day; residents may book five days ahead and non-residents three. The City's choice of those windows is the nearest thing it publishes to a statement of when its courts are wanted: mornings, and after four. Whenever nobody has booked them the reservable courts revert to drop-in play, and a drop-in player must leave "if a reservation holder arrives during their allotted time". The City adds that its sponsored programmes - the lessons it runs at all three parks and the Irvine Team Pickleball league at Portola Springs - have priority over all other use.

Court conditions

The City names no surface for any pickleball court in Irvine. No park page says what the courts are made of, the regulations page does not, and the pickleball page does not, so this site records nothing. What the City does write, consistently, is the word lit: "eight lit pickleball courts", "six lit pickleball courts" and "four lit pickleball courts" on the pickleball page, and "8 Lighted Pickleball Courts", "6 Lighted Pickleball Courts" and "4 Lighted Pickleball Courts" in the amenity lists, so the lighting of every published court in this city rests on two City records rather than one. The other thing the City is careful about is the difference between a pickleball court and a tennis court with pickleball on it. The eighteen courts here are all counted as pickleball courts. Separately, the City lists hybrid tennis-and-pickleball courts at a set of parks that includes Heritage, and gives only a city-wide total for them - ten on the pickleball page, nine on the regulations page - never a per-park figure, so none is added to any venue here. On a tennis court the regulations are explicit: "Tennis courts are designated solely for tennis use", pickleball may be played only when no tennis player is waiting, and if tennis players arrive "those playing pickleball or badminton must vacate the court". On the eighteen dedicated courts no such rule applies. Tennis shoes are required on every City court, and pets, food, bicycles and skateboards are not permitted on them.

The Irvine pickleball scene

Eighteen verified courts across three parks, every one lit in the City's own word, every park open 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. daily. Irvine is the first California city on this site. It is also the second operator here, after Mesa, to publish an hourly price for reserving a court, and the first to publish two of them: Courts #3 and #4 at Mike Ward Community Park and Courts #5 and #6 at Portola Springs Community Park can be booked at "Resident Rate: $17/hr" and "Non-Resident Rate: $19/hr". The other fourteen courts are first come, first served and the City states no price for them. The word free appears on none of its pages, so no Irvine venue reaches the free page. The City runs junior, adult and private lessons at all three parks through contract instructors, and a league, Irvine Team Pickleball, at Portola Springs: eight teams, a seven-week round robin and three-week playoffs, self-rated at 3.5, $490 per team per season. A fourth venue is missing, and it is missing because the City contradicts itself. The pickleball page and the regulations page both give Los Olivos Community Park "two lit pickleball courts"; the park's own amenity list gives "3 Lighted Pickleball Courts". This site's rule is that the record stating a number publishes, and that rule cannot choose between two numbers, so Los Olivos is named here and not listed. The City's pages disagree about the citywide total as well. The regulations page says "The City of Irvine has 96 public tennis courts, 12 public pickleball courts, and 8 public hybrid tennis/pickleball courts"; the per-park sentence on the pickleball page adds to twenty, and the regulations page's own list of lit courts leaves Heritage out altogether. Knollcrest, San Carlo, Turtle Rock and University carry hybrid courts under a city-wide aggregate with no per-park count, so they are not venues here. The City also says it "is evaluating building pickleball courts at the Great Park".
