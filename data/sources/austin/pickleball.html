Pickleball

What is Pickleball?

Pickleball is a paddle sport created for all ages and skill levels. The rules are simple, and the game is easy for beginners to learn, but it can become a quick, fast-paced, competitive game for experienced players. It's a game for everyone! Pickleball has evolved from original handmade equipment and simple rules into a popular sport throughout the US and Canada.

The Basics

  1. A fun sport that combines elements of tennis, badminton, and table tennis.
  2. Played indoors or outdoors on a badminton-sized court and a slightly modified tennis net.
  3. Players use a paddle and a plastic ball similar to a wiffle ball.

Pickleball was invented in 1965 when three dads, Joel Pritchard, Bill Bell, and Barney McCallum, created the game when their kids were bored with their usual summertime activities.

The Court

A pickleball court is the same size as a doubles badminton court and measures 20 by 44 feet. In pickleball, the same court is used for both singles and doubles play.

The court is striped similar to a tennis court with right and left service courts and a 7-foot non-volley zone in front of the net, referred to as the “kitchen."

Pickleball at Austin Parks and Recreation

    City of Austin Court Rules and Code of Conduct

    The following rules and code of conduct are in place to maximize the enjoyment of the sport at City of Austin facilities and to support shared use of space for all visitors.

    • Do not monopolize courts.
    • Unless otherwise noted, playing time is available on a first-come, first-served basis.
    • If others are waiting for a court, do not exceed 60 minutes of play for tennis or pickleball. If no one is waiting for the court, play may continue.
    • No individual may occupy multiple courts at one time.
    • No tournament play or for-profit lessons will be allowed unless approved by the Parks and Recreation Department.
    • Multi-purpose courts are intended for tennis and pickleball play only. No skateboards, rollerblades, bikes, or other activities unless approved by the Parks and Recreation Department.
    • Tennis courts without pickleball striping are intended for tennis play only. No pickleball play or informal striping is allowed on tennis courts. No skateboards, rollerblades, bikes, or other activities unless previously approved by the Parks and Recreation Department.
    • Dedicated pickleball courts are intended for pickleball play only. No tennis on pickleball courts. No skateboards, rollerblades, bikes, or other activities unless previously approved by the Parks and Recreation Department.
    • Marking of courts with paint or tape is not authorized and is considered defacing property. Tape adhesive can cause permanent damage to courts.
    • Altering existing non-adjustable nets or installing additional nets is not authorized and is considered defacing property.
