==========================================================================

1. THE WICHITA CORRECTION - I WAS WRONG IN V2.
   V2 said: a small number of flagship city pages could capture the national

   THEREFORE: that 85,000/mo is split across every city in the country. There

2. PICKLEHEADS IS THE LEADER, NOT PLAYPICKLEBALL.

   PlayPickleball: 25,245 pages, ~43,000 US visits/mo, 1.7 visits per page.
   PlayPickleball is your SEO template (URL and page architecture).
   Pickleheads is your product template (what the page should feel like).

3. GEAR IS THE REVENUE ENGINE - V2 TOLD YOU TO DEFER IT. REVERSED.
   Pickleheads' single biggest page is not a court page. It is
   /pickleball-gear/pickleball-paddles at 8,212 visits/mo. Gear reviews take
   5 of their top 12 pages.
   Courts drive retention. Affiliate gear drives revenue. Plan both from day
   one, even if gear ships in phase 8.

4. FILTER PAGES ARE REAL INDEXABLE URLS - V2 USED QUERY PARAMETERS. REVERSED.
   Exactly five indexable filters per city: /indoor/, /outdoor/, /free/,
   /public/, /lights/. Everything else stays a noindex query parameter.

5. URL PATTERN LOCKED BEFORE LAUNCH.
   /pickleball/us/{state}/{city}/{venue}/ - decided in Phase 0, 301s forever
   after. No exceptions, no later "improvements".

6. WORD COUNTS LOWERED TO REALISTIC BANDS.
   City 1,200-2,000 | Venue 700-1,200 | County 900-1,500 | Filter 600-1,000 |
   State 3,000-5,000. V2 was inflated.

7. LAUNCH SEQUENCE IS NOW THE SPINE OF THE PLAN.
   50-100 metros with complete data first. Prove the template ranks. Then
   scale to thousands. All three main competitors scaled before proving,
   which is exactly why 20,000+ pages produce so little for two of them.

8. DESCRIPTION RULE: TEMPLATE THE SENTENCE, NOT THE PARAGRAPH.
   That is the difference between Pickleheads' readable copy and
   CourtSource's obvious AI notes.

9. ONE QUERY FOR EVERY COUNT.
   All venue/court counts come from one shared module. CourtSource ships two
   pages claiming 45 vs 46 venues and 185 vs 187 courts on the same city.

10. SIX QUALITY GATES.
   Every page passes six explicit gates before it is allowed to go live.

SECTION 2 - ONE-PAGE SUMMARY
==========================================================================

THE OPPORTUNITY
Pickleball court search is a large, growing, evergreen, location-based
market. The incumbents are beatable for specific, documented reasons:
- CourtSource does ~9,236 US visits/mo on DR 8 with an entirely spam
  backlink profile. They rank on page quality alone. That means you can too.
- PlayPickleball has 25,245 pages and 1.7 visits per page. Volume without
  quality.
- Pickleheads is genuinely strong but has hard 404s on pages worth 1,475 and
  463 visits/mo, thin 656-word court pages, no BreadcrumbList /
  AggregateRating / Dataset schema, a signup wall on games and groups, and
  shows "0 nearby courts, 0 local coaches" to any non-US visitor.
- Nobody serves non-US markets properly.

THE STRATEGY IN ONE SENTENCE
Build a small number of metros to a quality standard none of them meet,
prove the template ranks, then scale the exact same template nationwide -
and monetise with affiliate gear content, not display ads.

THE THREE NUMBERS THAT MATTER
- Traffic per page: target 3.0+ (Pickleheads 3.0, PlayPickleball 1.7,
  CourtSource 0.45)
- Single-URL dependency: ceiling 15% (CourtSource 73%, PlayPickleball 28%,
  Pickleheads 6%)
- Verified venues per city page: minimum 3, or the page does not exist

THE NON-NEGOTIABLES
- Complete content in raw HTML with JavaScript disabled (only
  PlayPickleball fails this test; do not join them)
- Never buy a link (CourtSource has 1,345 referring domains and all of it is
  junk; whatyoudink has 343 referring domains and 1 visit/mo)
- Never publish a number without a source URL and a date-checked field
- Never render a null as 0 or "N/A" - render an honest gap with a
  "help us verify this" link
- No signup wall on anything indexable

SECTION 6 - KEYWORD ARCHITECTURE AND THE SEO PLAN
==========================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
6a. THE FIVE KEYWORD LAYERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

LAYER 1 - THE LOCALISED HEAD TERM
"pickleball courts near me" and its variants, roughly 85,000/mo nationally.
Do NOT target this with one page. It resolves to a different city for every
searcher. You win slices of it by having a strong page for each city. This
is the single most important strategic correction in v3.

LAYER 2 - CITY TERMS (your bread and butter)
Patterns:
  pickleball courts {city}
  pickleball courts in {city}
  pickleball {city} {state-abbrev}
  indoor pickleball courts {city}
  free pickleball courts {city}
  public pickleball courts {city}
Volume per city ranges from about 50 to several thousand. KD is typically
0-15. This is where a new site with no links can rank, which is exactly
what CourtSource proves at DR 8.
Target: one city page per metro, plus up to five indexable filter pages.

LAYER 3 - VENUE NAME TERMS (the cheapest wins in the whole niche)
Real examples pulled from the competitor exports, with monthly volume:
  winter haven tennis center            200
  martz field                           150
  pickleball courts jacksonville fl     150
  winter haven tennis courts            100
  waldstein park brookline               50
  erie tennis courts                     50
Each one is tiny. There are tens of thousands of them. KD is effectively
zero because the only competition is the venue's own thin page or nothing.
A venue page that is genuinely better than the parks department PDF wins.
This layer is how you get from zero to your first few thousand visits.

LAYER 4 - COUNTY AND STATE TERMS
  pickleball courts in {state}
  pickleball courts {county} county
Lower volume, but they are the hub pages that pass internal link equity
down to cities and venues, and they rank for the broad research queries.

LAYER 5 - GEAR AND INFORMATIONAL TERMS (the money layer)
Pickleheads' top page is /pickleball-gear/pickleball-paddles at 8,212
visits/mo, and gear takes 5 of their top 12 pages. Also in this layer:
  best pickleball paddles
  pickleball paddles for beginners
  pickleball rules
  how to play pickleball
  pickleball court dimensions
  pickleball vs tennis
Courts drive retention. Gear drives revenue. Build courts first because
they are your defensible moat, but do not postpone gear past phase 8.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
6b. ONE PRIMARY KEYWORD PER URL - ENFORCED AT BUILD TIME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
Maintain keyword-map.json. Every page type maps to exactly one primary
keyword and up to three secondaries. The build fails if two URLs claim the
same primary keyword. This single rule prevents the cannibalisation that is
strangling PlayPickleball's 5,751 city pages.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
6c. TITLE AND META FORMULAS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
City page
  Title: Pickleball Courts in {City}, {ST} - {N} Verified Locations ({Year})
  Meta:  Find all {N} pickleball courts in {City}, {ST}. Indoor and outdoor,
         free and paid, with hours, surface type and lighting. Updated
         {Month Year}.

Venue page
  Title: {Venue Name} - Pickleball Courts in {City}, {ST}
  Meta:  {N} pickleball courts at {Venue Name} in {City}. {Surface},
         {indoor/outdoor}, {lights/no lights}. Hours, fees and directions.

Filter page
  Title: Indoor Pickleball Courts in {City}, {ST} - {N} Locations
  Meta:  {N} indoor pickleball courts in {City}, {ST}. Play year-round.
         Hours, fees and booking details for each facility.

County page
  Title: Pickleball Courts in {County} County, {ST} - {N} Locations
State page
  Title: Pickleball Courts in {State} - {N} Locations Across {M} Cities

RULES
- Year token only where the content is genuinely refreshed annually
- Never let a title exceed 60 characters after token substitution; the
  build should warn
- Counts in titles come from the same shared count module as the body

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
6d. WHAT NOT TO DO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Do not create a city page for a city with fewer than 3 verified venues
- Do not create a filter page unless 3+ venues match that filter
- Do not buy links, guest post at scale, or touch a PBN. CourtSource has
  1,345 spam referring domains and 9,236 visits. whatyoudink has 343 and
  1 visit. The correlation is zero.
- Do not chase "pickleball courts near me" with a landing page

SECTION 8 - TEMPLATE SPECIFICATION
==========================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
8a. THE SIX DECISIONS YOU MAKE ONCE, IN PHASE 0, AND NEVER REVISIT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

DECISION 1 - URL PATTERN, LOCKED BEFORE LAUNCH
  /pickleball/us/{state}/{city}/{venue}/
  /pickleball/us/{state}/{city}/
  /pickleball/us/{state}/{city}/{indoor|outdoor|free|public|lights}/
  /pickleball/us/{state}/{county}-county/
  /pickleball/us/{state}/
  /pickleball-gear/{category}/
  The "us" segment exists so phase 11 can add /ca/, /au/, /uk/ without a
  migration. Once live, every change is a 301 that you maintain forever.
  Write this into decisions.md and treat it as immutable.

DECISION 2 - EVERY COUNT COMES FROM ONE QUERY
  A single module, getCounts(scope), returns venue count, court count,
  indoor count, free count and lit count. Titles, meta, headings, body and
  schema all call it. No page ever computes its own number.
  Why: CourtSource ships 45-vs-46 and 185-vs-187 contradictions on the same
  city. It is the fastest way to look untrustworthy.

DECISION 3 - TEMPLATE THE SENTENCE, NOT THE PARAGRAPH
  Allowed: a sentence with slots - "{Venue} has {N} {surface} courts,
  {lit or unlit}, and is {free or paid}."
  Not allowed: a whole paragraph of boilerplate with a city name swapped in.
  Each page needs at least three sentences of genuinely specific,
  non-templatable detail: a parking note, a peak-hours note, a surface
  condition note, a local quirk.
  This is the exact line between Pickleheads' readable copy and
  CourtSource's AI notes.

DECISION 4 - FIVE INDEXABLE FILTERS, EVERYTHING ELSE NOINDEX
  Indexable, real URLs: /indoor/, /outdoor/, /free/, /public/, /lights/
  Everything else (surface, court count, sort order, distance radius) is a
  query parameter with noindex. Five is the whole list. Do not add a sixth
  without deleting one.

DECISION 5 - WORD BANDS, ENFORCED BY THE BUILD
  City page      1,200 - 2,000 words
  Venue page       700 - 1,200 words
  County page      900 - 1,500 words
  Filter page      600 - 1,000 words
  State page     3,000 - 5,000 words
  Under the floor means the page does not publish. Pickleheads' court pages
  sit at 656 words; your floor of 700 is deliberately just above it.

DECISION 6 - HONEST GAPS, NEVER FAKE ZEROS
  A null renders as "Not verified yet" with a "help us verify this" link -
  never as 0, never as "N/A", never as a guess. Every published fact carries
  a source URL and a date-checked value, both visible on the page.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
8b. THE SIX QUALITY GATES - EVERY PAGE PASSES ALL SIX OR IT DOES NOT SHIP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

GATE 1 - DATA THRESHOLD
  3 or more verified venues for a city, county or filter page to exist.
  A venue page requires a verified address and at least one verified court
  count.

GATE 2 - JAVASCRIPT-OFF RENDER
  Fetch the raw HTML. All primary content, all internal links and all
  schema must be present. If it needs JS, it fails. PlayPickleball fails
  this test; it is the only one of the three that does.

GATE 3 - SCHEMA COMPLETENESS
  Required per page type: BreadcrumbList everywhere, SportsActivityLocation
  or LocalBusiness on venues, ItemList on city and filter pages,
  AggregateRating where real ratings exist, Dataset on state pages.
  Must validate clean. No competitor ships all of this.

GATE 4 - WORD BAND AND SPECIFICITY
  Inside the band from Decision 5, and containing at least three
  non-templatable specific sentences.

GATE 5 - COUNT CONSISTENCY
  Every number on the page traces to getCounts(). An automated check
  compares title, meta, H1, body and schema counts and fails on mismatch.

GATE 6 - SOURCE AND FRESHNESS
  Every factual claim has a source URL and a date-checked field. Nothing
  older than the review cadence is displayed without a stale flag.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
8c. PAGE ANATOMY - CITY PAGE (1,200-2,000 words)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. H1: Pickleball Courts in {City}, {ST}
2. Lead paragraph with the verified count, the last-updated date, and one
   genuinely local sentence
3. Quick-answer block: total venues, total courts, how many indoor, how
   many free, how many lit
4. The five filter links (indoor, outdoor, free, public, lights) as real
   internal links to real pages
5. Venue table: name, courts, surface, indoor/outdoor, lights, fee, link
6. Venue cards below the table with the specific detail
7. "Best for beginners / best for competitive play / best when it rains"
   editorial section
8. Local context: peak times, seasonality, parking, local league or club
9. FAQ block with 5-8 real questions, marked up as FAQPage
10. Nearby cities and the parent county and state links
11. Sources and date-checked footer

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
8d. PAGE ANATOMY - VENUE PAGE (700-1,200 words)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. H1: {Venue Name} - Pickleball Courts in {City}, {ST}
2. Fact panel: courts, surface, indoor/outdoor, lights, nets, fee, hours,
   reservation policy, address, source URL, date checked
3. Two to three paragraphs of specific, human description
4. Getting there: parking, transit, access notes
5. What to expect: peak hours, skill level, open play schedule
6. Nearby alternatives - three other venues in the same city
7. Breadcrumb up to city, county, state
8. FAQ if there are real questions
9. Sources and date-checked footer

SECTION 9 - DATA MODEL
==========================================================================

VENUE
  id, slug, name, aliases[]
  address_line, city, county, state, postal_code, country
  lat, lng
  court_count, surface, indoor (bool), lights (bool), nets_provided (bool)
  fee_type (free | paid | member), fee_notes

CITY

  venue_ids[], computed counts via getCounts()
  editorial_intro, local_context, faq[]
  last_reviewed

COUNTY / STATE
  slug, name, child_ids[], editorial body, last_reviewed

GEAR ITEM (phase 8)
  slug, name, category, brand, price_band, affiliate_url
  specs{}, verdict, pros[], cons[], best_for, date_tested

HARD RULES ON THE DATA
- Slug registry bans numeric duplicate suffixes. playyourcourt has
  martz-field-2, waldstein-park-2 and vista-ridge-2 live in production.
  Collisions must be resolved with real disambiguation
  (venue-name-neighbourhood), never with -2.
- Nulls stay null. No zero-filling, ever.
- date_checked is mandatory on every published fact.
- Counts are never stored denormalised on the page; always computed.

SECTION 10 - THE BUILD: PHASE BY PHASE (0 through 12)
==========================================================================

THE SHAPE OF THE WHOLE THING
Phases 0-2 are foundation. Phase 3-6 build and prove ONE metro, then 50-100.
Phase 7 is the proof checkpoint - you do not pass it until the template
ranks. Phases 8-12 are scale and monetisation. The checkpoint at phase 7 is
the single most important gate in this document, because skipping it is the
exact mistake all three competitors made.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 0 - DECISIONS AND SKELETON (week 1)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
GOAL: Nothing is built. Everything is decided and written down.
DO:
- Buy the domain. Prefer something that reads as a directory, not a brand
  you will have to explain.
- Stack: Next.js with static generation, server-rendered HTML, file-based
  or lightweight DB content, deployed on a platform with instant rollback.
  The only hard requirement is that pages render fully without JS.

SECTION D - THE CLAIM LAYER (new, because your product has one)
==========================================================================

Your claimed_or_verified column is doing two incompatible jobs. Split it:

  claimed_by_owner        bool     an owner completed the claim flow
  claim_date              date
  claimed_by_contact      string   internal only, never rendered
  data_verified           bool     derived: source_url AND date_checked
                                   both present and inside cadence
  verified_by             enum     municipal_source | owner_submission |
                                   staff_check | user_report

An owner-claimed listing is a source, and a good one - but it is an

you have built a mechanism where anyone who claims a venue can write

and Gate 6 failing simultaneously, silently, at scale.

  1. Verified from municipal source, {date_checked}
  2. Confirmed by the venue, {claim_date}
  3. Listed from public data, not yet verified   [help us verify this]
  4. Not verified yet                            [help us verify this]

over an unclaimed one. The moment claiming buys position, the directory is
an ad product and the editorial promise is gone. Claiming buys the owner
control of THEIR facts, a response channel, and nothing else.

SECTION G - getCounts(scope), REVISED FOR YOUR SCHEMA
==========================================================================

Decision 2 stands unchanged: one module, no page computes its own number.
Your columns let it return more, and the split between venue-level and
court-level counts must be explicit or your titles will contradict your
tables.

getCounts(scope) returns:
  venues                 count of published venues in scope
  courts                 SUM(total_courts) where verified
  indoor_courts          SUM(indoor_courts)
  outdoor_courts         SUM(outdoor_courts)
  venues_indoor          venues with indoor_courts > 0
  venues_outdoor         venues with outdoor_courts > 0
  venues_free            venues with fee_type = free
  venues_lit             venues with light = true
  venues_covered         venues with covered = true
  venues_climate         venues with climate_control = true
  venues_unverified      published venues lacking full verification

Two hard rules on top:
  - A count NEVER silently includes rows where the underlying field is
    null. getCounts returns the count AND the denominator it was drawn
    from, so the page can say "12 of 15 venues report lighting" instead

  - venues_unverified is rendered, not hidden. Publishing your own gap
    count is the trust move none of the competitors will make.

Title formulas in Section 6c say "{N} Verified Locations". With this
dataset, N is the VERIFIED venue count, not the imported row count. If a
city has 22 rows and 6 verified, the title says 6. Never 22.

SECTION 1 - CHANGELOG: WHAT CHANGED IN V4 AND WHY
==========================================================================

1. THE DATASET ARRIVED, AND IT MOVES THE BOTTLENECK.
   18,038 venues, 33 columns. Section 3 of v3 flagged data cost as the one
   unmet criterion. That is resolved. But the dataset has no source_url and
   no date_checked column, which means Rule 7 and Gate 6 currently block
   every single row from publishing. The constraint did not disappear; it
   moved one step downstream, from "find venues" to "prove venues". That is
   a far better problem, because confirmation is roughly ten times faster
   than discovery.

2. PHASE 1 IS REWRITTEN. ACQUISITION BECOMES IMPORT, TRIAGE, VERIFY.
   Prompt P1 in v3 built a collection workflow. P1 in v4 builds an import
   mapper, a data-quality report, a county backfill and a verification work
   queue ordered by leverage. New block P1B runs the verification sprint.

3. DATA GATES ARE NOW SEPARATE FROM PAGE GATES.
   v3 had six quality gates, all of which fire at page-build time. With
   18,038 imported rows there needs to be an earlier checkpoint, at the
   row. Four IMPORT GATES now sit in front of the six page gates. See 8b.
   The six page gates are UNCHANGED and remain verbatim-immutable.

4. CLAIMED IS NOT VERIFIED. NEW DECISION 7.
   The listing product means owners will claim venues. A claim is an
   identity event; a verification is a provenance event. They never share a
   field. If a claim can flip the verified flag, then the headline verified
   count - the entire value proposition, exactly as findswimmingholes uses
   "1,216 Verified Places" - becomes whatever venue owners type in.

5. VERIFIED COUNTS ONLY IN TITLES. NEW DECISION 8.
   A city with 22 imported rows and 6 verified venues has a title that says
   6. The imported row count never appears anywhere a user or crawler can
   see it. Holding 18,038 rows creates constant pressure to publish the
   bigger number, which is precisely how PlayPickleball ended up with 5,750
   dead city pages.

6. COVERED AND CLIMATE-CONTROLLED ARE NOT INDOOR.
   The dataset distinguishes indoor_courts, covered and climate_control.
   Only indoor_courts drives the /indoor/ filter. A covered outdoor court
   is not year-round play in a cold state, and saying otherwise is the kind
   of small inaccuracy that destroys the trust the directory sells.

7. ACCESS TYPE SPLITS INTO TWO AXES.
   The source column mixes free, drop-in and fee-based. Free versus paid is
   PRICE. Drop-in versus reservation is FORMAT. Split into fee_type and
   play_format. The /free/ filter reads fee_type only - a drop-in session
   with a door fee is not free.

8. TWO RATING FIELDS FORCE AN AGGREGATERATING DECISION.
   rating and user_rating cannot both be authoritative. Third-party ratings
   display with attribution and are excluded from schema. Only ratings
   collected on our own site may be emitted, above a review_count floor.
   Expect almost no AggregateRating markup at launch. That is correct, and
   it still beats three competitors who ship none.

9. getCounts() RETURNS DENOMINATORS, NOT JUST NUMERATORS.
   With this much null-bearing data, "3 venues have lights" implies the
   rest do not. The module now returns the population each count was drawn
   from, so pages can say "12 of 15 venues report lighting". It also
   returns venues_unverified, and that number is rendered, not hidden.

10. COUNTY IS A BLOCKING GAP, NOT A NICE-TO-HAVE.
   The locked URL pattern contains a county level. The dataset has no
   county column. It must be derived from lat/lng with postal_code
   fallback, with per-row confidence, before Phase 4 can exist at all.

11. THE CORRECTION CHANNEL IS A COMPETITIVE EDGE, NOT OVERHEAD.
   None of the three competitors offer a correction route worth using. The
   Decision 6 help-us-verify link, the owner claim flow and Deep Pickleball
   support all resolve to one queue, and every item in it is inbound Gate 6
   material with a source and a date attached.

12. EVERYTHING ELSE FROM V3 STANDS.
   The Wichita correction, the URL pattern, the five filters, the word
   bands, the sequencing rule, the six page gates, the no-bought-links
   rule, gear as the revenue layer. None of it is reopened by having data.
